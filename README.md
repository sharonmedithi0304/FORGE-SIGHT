**How can quality signals, process context, and potential economic consequences be examined together to support better engineering investigations?**

## ` THE MISSION `

### Don't just see the defect. Understand the bigger picture.

</div>

Manufacturing quality problems rarely exist in isolation.

A rise in reported defects can be a warning sign. A process bottleneck can add important context. An operational constraint can influence the investigation. A quality issue can also carry costs that extend beyond the defective unit itself.

Yet these perspectives are often examined separately.

**ForgeSight brings quality investigation, process intelligence, evidence review, and economic-impact assessment into one software experience.**

The goal is to make the path from an observed problem to a structured investigation clearer, more traceable, and more useful.

Rather than presenting every correlation as a cause or every estimate as a fact, ForgeSight emphasizes a distinction that matters in engineering:

> **What do we know? What do we suspect? What still needs to be validated?**

That distinction is central to building industrial software that people can reason about, question, and trust.

# `01` — THE PROBLEM SPACE

## When the signals are connected, the investigation gets clearer.

Imagine a production batch where surface-crack reports begin increasing.

The quality signal deserves attention. But the defect count alone does not explain the entire situation.

- Is the observed pattern unusual compared with the reference batch?
- Which evidence is available to support the investigation?
- What process conditions deserve closer examination?
- Are there operational constraints that could provide additional context?
- What could the issue mean for throughput, rework, waste, or other business costs?
- Which conclusions are supported by evidence, and which remain hypotheses?

These questions belong to a broader investigation rather than a single chart.

### The challenge

| Fragmented perspective | What the investigation needs |
|---|---|
| A defect count without context | A quality signal with relevant comparisons |
| Process information viewed separately | Process context linked to the question being investigated |
| A suspected explanation treated as a fact | Clear separation between observation and hypothesis |
| Costs considered only after the event | An explicit view of potential economic implications |
| Conclusions without visible assumptions | Reasoning that can be examined and challenged |

ForgeSight is designed around this wider perspective.

It is not simply about displaying information. It is about arranging the information so that a person can ask better questions.

## 2.1 — Command Center

**The operational picture, brought into focus.**

The Command Center is the entry point for examining the current investigation scenario.

The repository includes a scenario involving an increase in surface-crack reports associated with batch `B-2048`. The page brings together quality indicators, affected-unit context, comparisons, supporting evidence, process information, and estimated economic consequences.

The purpose is to provide a wider starting point for investigation.

The Command Center brings attention to questions such as:

- What quality issue is currently under examination?
- Which measurements describe the reported signal?
- What does the comparison batch suggest?
- Which evidence associations should be reviewed?
- What operational information could be relevant?
- Which economic-impact figures are estimates rather than directly observed measurements?

A useful command center should help a user understand the situation before jumping to a conclusion.

## 2.2 — Investigation Center

**Move from an alert to a structured investigation.**

The Investigation Center focuses on the context surrounding the issue.

A meaningful investigation requires more than recognizing that a number has changed. It requires keeping the question, available evidence, and possible explanations organized.

The investigation experience is intended to support that process by bringing together:

- The current investigation context.
- The observed quality signal.
- Relevant evidence associations.
- Process and operational context.
- Potential economic implications.
- A clearer distinction between available information and conclusions that still require validation.

The principle is simple: **an investigation should preserve the difference between what was observed and what someone thinks it means.**

## 2.3 — Quality Intelligence

**A measurement is the beginning of a question, not necessarily its answer.**

Quality indicators can help establish the size, location, or character of a problem. Comparisons can provide additional context and help identify patterns that deserve closer attention.

ForgeSight's quality-oriented experience is intended to help users examine such signals within a broader investigation.

Important questions include:

- What exactly is being measured?
- What is the relevant baseline or comparison?
- Is the comparison meaningful for this scenario?
- Is the available information sufficient to support the interpretation?
- What additional evidence would strengthen or weaken the hypothesis?

These questions prevent an attractive dashboard from becoming a source of false certainty.

## 2.4 — Process Intelligence

**Quality does not exist independently of operations.**

Production conditions, resource constraints, throughput, capacity, utilization, and queues may provide relevant context when examining a quality issue.

ForgeSight includes process-related information in its investigation experience so that operational context can be considered alongside the observed signal.

This does not mean that a process indicator automatically explains a defect.

Instead, process context helps identify the next questions to investigate.

A responsible interpretation must consider timing, measurement quality, alternative explanations, and whether the evidence supports the proposed relationship.

## 2.5 — Economic Impact

**Understand the possible cost of a problem, not just the number of defects.**

An investigation may have consequences beyond the quality metric itself. Depending on the available data and assumptions, these can include rework, waste, production disruption, or other operational costs.

An economic-impact view helps bring the business dimension into the same conversation.

However, estimated impact must not be confused with audited financial loss.

A useful economic assessment should make clear:

- Which inputs are directly observed.
- Which values are estimated.
- Which assumptions influence the result.
- Whether the calculation represents a scenario or an actual recorded cost.
- How sensitive the result is to changes in those assumptions.

The objective is to make the potential implications easier to examine—not to create an illusion of financial precision.

## Stage 01 — Observe

Identify the reported issue and establish the context of the signal.

A useful starting point includes the measurement, the affected scope, the relevant time or batch context, and any available reference comparison.

**Output:** a clearly defined issue to investigate.

## Stage 02 — Investigate

Gather and examine the information associated with the issue.

Look for supporting evidence, relevant comparisons, and missing information. A hypothesis should be traceable to the observations that motivated it.

**Output:** an organized set of evidence and investigation questions.

## Stage 03 — Contextualize

Consider the surrounding operational picture.

Process indicators can suggest which areas deserve examination, but they must be interpreted carefully. A simultaneous change does not establish a causal relationship.

**Output:** relevant context and potential explanations to test.

## Stage 04 — Assess

Consider the potential operational and economic consequences.

Where estimated values are used, their assumptions and limitations should remain visible.

**Output:** an impact assessment that can be reviewed rather than accepted blindly.

## Stage 05 — Validate

Return to the evidence.

Ask whether the proposed explanation is supported, whether alternative explanations remain plausible, and what further measurements or tests are required.

**Output:** a more defensible basis for engineering judgement.

> **Important:** This loop describes the intended investigation methodology. It should not be interpreted as a claim that the application automatically proves root causes or validates every hypothesis.

# `04` — ENGINEERING PRINCIPLES

<div align="center">

<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/EVIDENCE_FIRST-22D3EE?style=for-the-badge&labelColor=101820" alt="Evidence First" />
<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/TRACEABLE_REASONING-818CF8?style=for-the-badge&labelColor=101820" alt="Traceable Reasoning" />
<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/HUMAN_JUDGEMENT-34D399?style=for-the-badge&labelColor=101820" alt="Human Judgement" />

</div>

### 4.1 — Observations are not assumptions

Observed measurements and supplied inputs form the basis of an investigation. Derived values depend on calculations. Estimates depend on assumptions. Predictions, where present, depend on a model and its limitations.

These categories should not be mixed together.

### 4.2 — A hypothesis is not a confirmed cause

Finding two indicators together does not prove that one caused the other.

Causal conclusions require appropriate evidence and validation. ForgeSight's decision-support approach should preserve that distinction.

### 4.3 — Numbers need context

A percentage without a denominator, a cost without assumptions, or a comparison without a meaningful baseline can be misleading.

Measurements should be interpreted in relation to their scope, source, units, and limitations.

### 4.4 — Industrial software should respect operational boundaries

ForgeSight is a software-only decision-support project. Its role is to help people inspect information and investigate potential problems—not to directly operate industrial equipment.

### 4.5 — Clarity is part of correctness

A polished interface should not conceal uncertainty.

Labels, comparisons, and explanatory context should help a reviewer understand what a value means and what it does not mean.

**The development server does not start**

Read the package manager's error message and confirm that the Node.js version is compatible with the project.

Run the build command again and inspect the TypeScript or bundler errors. Fix the reported issue instead of assuming that a successful development server guarantees a production build.

**A page or feature appears incomplete**

Check the corresponding page component, the shared application shell, and the route or navigation behavior before making changes.

Additional environment variables or external services should only be configured if the implementation requires them.

# `09` — DATA, ASSUMPTIONS & INTERPRETATION

Industrial analytics is only useful when its limitations are understood.

ForgeSight's interface and documentation should preserve the following distinctions.

<div align="center">

<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/OBSERVED-Available_Evidence-22D3EE?style=for-the-badge&labelColor=101820" alt="Observed evidence" />

<br/>

<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/DERIVED-Calculated_From_Inputs-818CF8?style=for-the-badge&labelColor=101820" alt="Derived values" />

<br/>

<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/ESTIMATED-Assumption_Dependent-F59E0B?style=for-the-badge&labelColor=101820" alt="Estimated values" />

<br/>

<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/PREDICTED-Model_Dependent-FF6B8A?style=for-the-badge&labelColor=101820" alt="Predicted values" />

<br/>

<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/SIMULATED-Scenario_Based-A78BFA?style=for-the-badge&labelColor=101820" alt="Simulated values" />

</div>

### Observed

Information explicitly supplied by the source data or recorded measurements.

### Derived

Values calculated from other inputs using a defined calculation.

### Estimated

Values dependent on assumptions, approximate inputs, or estimation methods.

### Predicted

Outputs generated by a predictive model, where such a model exists and is used.

### Simulated

Results from a constructed scenario rather than a directly observed production event.

These labels are not interchangeable. A derived value can be calculated precisely while still depending on incomplete or inaccurate inputs. A prediction can be useful without being certain. A simulated scenario can inform planning without representing a real event.

### Questions to ask before accepting a result

- What is the source of this value?
- What unit and time period does it represent?
- Which inputs were used in the calculation?
- Which assumptions could materially change the result?
- Does the comparison use a suitable baseline?
- What evidence would be required to validate the conclusion?

These questions should remain part of the investigation, not an afterthought.

# `10` — SOFTWARE-ONLY BY DESIGN

<div align="center">

<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/CONTROL_BOUNDARY-ADVISORY_ONLY-F59E0B?style=for-the-badge&labelColor=101820" alt="Advisory only" />

</div>

ForgeSight is intended as a software-only analysis and decision-support experience.

Its purpose is to help people review information, examine patterns, and structure investigations.

It is **not** presented as a direct industrial-control system.

- It does not claim to directly operate PLCs or robotic equipment.
- It does not claim to control production machinery.
- It does not establish root cause merely because two indicators appear related.
- It does not turn an estimate into a verified financial result.
- It should not be treated as a substitute for engineering validation or established safety procedures.

Any future integration with real operational systems would require explicit interface definitions, appropriate access controls, validation, and a clearly documented control boundary.

**Decision support should improve human judgement, not obscure responsibility.**

# `13` — WHY FORGESIGHT?

Industrial systems generate information. Engineering teams need ways to interpret that information in context.

ForgeSight explores the space between an observed quality issue and an informed investigation.

Its central ideas are straightforward:

**Look beyond the metric.**

A number is more useful when its context, comparison, and limitations are understood.

**Follow the evidence.**

A plausible explanation is only a starting point until the available evidence supports it.

**Connect operations and impact.**

Quality and operational context can inform a broader understanding of a problem.

**Keep assumptions visible.**

Trustworthy decision support should make uncertainty easier to see, not easier to ignore.

**Leave the final judgement accountable.**

The application should support the people making decisions, with appropriate validation and human oversight.

# `14` — ABOUT THIS PROJECT

ForgeSight is an evolving industrial software project focused on quality investigation, process context, and economic-impact analysis.

It combines a modular frontend with an investigation-oriented product concept: helping users move beyond isolated indicators and examine the wider context of a potential operational problem.

The project is developed with an emphasis on:

- Structured interface design.
- Modular React and TypeScript development.
- Data interpretation and visualization.
- Evidence-aware investigation workflows.
